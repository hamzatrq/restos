# 11 — Staff & People

**Module spec — Draft 1, July 2026** · Parent: `00-platform-overview.md` (conventions §5–§7 inherited), `01-kernel-sync.md` (auth/PIN sessions, event contracts). Concept doc §4.5 (people plane) and §5 (payroll computation explicitly out). Wave 3.

## 1. Purpose & scope

The people plane: attendance, the advances/baqaya ledger, basic shift scheduling, and restaurant memory (opening/closing checklists, shift handover notes, SOP/recipe-card storage — the restaurant survives any one person leaving).

- **Explicitly not:** salary computation or payroll processing (export only — concept doc §5), HR workflows, hiring, biometric identification.
- **Who uses it:** every staff member (clock-in/out, own record self-view), branch manager (schedules, checklist oversight, handovers), owner (ledger, exports, completion visibility via docs 12/14), external accountant (CSV exports).
- **Runs on:** cloud service + surfaces embedded in existing branch devices (POS doc 02, pass/KDS doc 03, manager console doc 05 — no separate staff app) + back office (doc 14) + owner app (doc 12).
- **Tiers/profiles:** all tiers, all profiles.
- **Framing (`restaurant-os.md` Appendix A, binding):** every feature doubles as protection *for* the staff member — attendance provable, advance balance provable, "I completed my checklist" provable. This is the adoption unlock.

## 2. Position in platform

- **Events consumed:** kernel PIN sessions (01-F26/F28 — attendance rides on them), `shift.opened / closed`, `day.opened / closed`, `cash.paid_out` (drawer-sourced advances).
- **Events emitted:** `staff.clocked_in / clocked_out / advance_recorded / advance_repaid` (01 §4) plus these extensions, **absorbed into the 01 §4 catalog July 2026**: `checklist.item_checked` / `checklist.completed` (11-F15), `handover.recorded` (11-F17), `staff.schedule_published` (11-F12), `staff.advance_acknowledged` (11-F10). (The earlier pointer to a list "in §5" was dangling — no such list existed, so these types lived only in FR prose and were therefore unimplementable under 01-F4.)
- **Serves:** doc 05 (checklist state, overtime and missed-clock-out alarms), doc 12 (attendance/advances/memory views), doc 13 (labor signals for the nightly brief), doc 14 (templates, schedule editing, exports).
- **Reference data:** checklist templates, shift presets, schedules, and SOP documents distribute to devices over the kernel reference-data channel (01 §8) — one replication path. **⚠ Amended August 2026: there is no frame these four can ride today.** `01-F75` closes that channel's resource set at `catalog` and `staff` and excludes these deliberately — none has a writer, a publication surface or a device consumer yet, so a declared member would be a name on the wire with nothing at either end. **Owed to THIS document:** each set is admitted by an amendment to `01-F75` plus its own golden fixture, at the point it acquires a writer (`11-F18` is the same clause one section down). Nothing here may be smuggled down a `reference_response` typed for another resource.

## 3. Functional requirements

**Attendance**
- 11-F1 Clock-in/out by PIN on any branch device. The first PIN unlock inside a scheduled shift window prompts a one-tap clock-in; an explicit clock tile is always available. *(Automation law: side-effect of the PIN session staff already use.)*
- 11-F2 Optional selfie at clock-in/out (org setting): a camera capture attached as evidence to the event. No face recognition — the photo is human-checkable evidence, not biometrics. If enabled it is required; a camera-failure skip is allowed but flagged.
- 11-F3 Fully offline-safe: clock events persist locally and sync later (00 §5.1–5.2); the staff member sees immediate confirmation.
- 11-F4 Overtime flag: actual clocked span exceeding the scheduled shift length plus a configured threshold is flagged in the read model and surfaced to docs 05/12. Derived — no data entry.
- 11-F5 Missed clock-out: auto-closed at `day.closed` with an `auto_closed` flag; manager confirmation next day is an append-only correction event; habitual misses are flagged per staff member.
- 11-F6 Self-view: after PIN unlock, any staff member can see their own attendance record and advance balance. Always on — not configurable (protection framing is platform law here).
- 11-F7 Payroll **export only**: CSV per period per branch — days present, shifts worked, flagged overtime spans, advances taken/repaid, outstanding balance. No wage rates, no salary math; the accountant computes pay. Salary computation is deliberately out of scope.

**Advances / baqaya ledger**
- 11-F8 An advance is recorded on-device with dual attribution in one flow: the granting role (per permission matrix) confirms, then the receiving staff member acknowledges with their own PIN. No advance exists without the staff acknowledgment — the on-device dual confirm *is* the hand-over. *(Side-effect of the cash hand-over both parties are performing anyway.)*
- 11-F9 Drawer-sourced advances ride `cash.paid_out` with category `staff_advance`, automatically linking a `staff.advance_recorded` — one action writes both the cash ledger and the staff ledger. *(Side-effect.)*
- 11-F10 Optional repayment schedule (amount per pay period). Repayments are recorded during the payroll-export ritual: the system proposes scheduled deductions, the owner confirms → `staff.advance_repaid`; the staff member is asked to acknowledge at their next PIN session (pending-acknowledgment badge → `staff.advance_acknowledged`). *(Scheduled verified ritual.)*
- 11-F11 Running balance per staff member, visible to the owner **and** to the staff member themselves ("my balance is provable"). Ledger is append-only; disputes create correction events carrying both parties' acknowledgments; history is never rewritten.

**Scheduling (basic)**
- 11-F12 Weekly schedule grid: staff × day × shift preset; week templates copy forward; publishing is explicit → `staff.schedule_published` (versioned; republish supersedes, prior versions retained).
- 11-F13 Staff see their own upcoming shifts on any branch device after PIN unlock; entries changed since last view are marked.
- 11-F14 A schedule is a plan (configuration), never an attendance fact — facts come only from clock events (11-F1). Deliberately absent: availability bidding, shift-swap marketplace, labor-cost optimization.

**Restaurant memory**
- 11-F15 Opening/closing checklists: per-branch templates (org-defined via doc 14); items are icon + short text; per-item check-off with optional photo proof (template marks which items require a photo) → `checklist.item_checked`, `checklist.completed`. Item checks ride the LAN fast path to the manager console. *(Scheduled verified ritual — the photo is the verification.)*
- 11-F16 Completion state visible to manager/owner (docs 05/12). `day.closed` with an incomplete opening/closing checklist is flagged, never blocked.
- 11-F17 Shift handover note at shift close, structured: cash handed over (prefilled from the shift-close expected/counted figures — never re-typed), pending issues (quick-tags + optional text or voice note), stock notes (quick-tags). Attached to the `shift.closed` event → `handover.recorded`. *(Side-effect of the shift-close ritual that already happens.)*
- 11-F18 SOP/recipe-card storage: versioned reference documents (PDF, images, short text) uploaded via back office; view-only on branch devices, organized by station; distributed over the reference-data channel. Not a data capture — reference distribution only.
- 11-F19 Low-literacy design (tightens 00 §5.6): every staff-facing action ≤ 3 taps, icon-led, English labels kept short and paired with icons; wherever free text exists, a voice-note alternative exists.

**The person record (August 2026 — this module's Wave is 3, but the record every other module attributes to is needed from Wave 1, so the minimum lands here now)**

- 11-F20 **A person is a NAMED record; the name is required, single across both planes, and never an identifier.** What the corpus already decides, so that this FR extends rather than restates: 01-F26 gives `User × Role × per-location assignment` with permission overrides and a PIN; 01-F28 gives offline PIN verification against synced credential hashes; **01-F61 already requires a `display_name` on the staff record** — "because the identification tile must render something" — plus a `grid_ordinal` and roster changes landing at the 01-F46 business-day boundary; 14-F14 owns the CRUD surface and rules that deactivation preserves historical attribution; 15-F26 creates the first one. What none of them says is that the name is **required on the one record both planes read** — and measured August 2026 it is on neither: the cloud user record carries `user_id`, `org_id`, `email`, a password hash and assignments with **no name at all**, the device roster's `display_name` is optional, and the till's staff grid is populated from a development constant.
  - **The required minimum, and no more:** `user_id` (UUIDv7, never reused — 01-F1 makes attribution permanent, so a recycled id reassigns somebody else's acts); `org_id`; a required, non-empty `display_name`; `assignments` (01-F26); the credential each plane needs (15-F26's email + password on the cloud plane, 01-F28's PIN hash on the device plane); and 01-F61's `grid_ordinal`. Deferred to the FRs that read them: attendance and the advances ledger (11-F1..11-F11, this module's own Wave 3), and a photo or fixed per-person mark — which **01-F61 already records as materially better than a name for this population** and puts on doc 27, so it is owed there and must not be invented here.
  - **ONE name, not one per plane.** The cloud user record and the device staff record are two projections of one person; a second name field is a second source for one fact, and the two disagree the first time somebody's name changes. The device projection may be a subset of the cloud record; it may not hold a name the cloud record does not.
  - **A name is NOT an identifier and nothing may key on it.** Two people legitimately share one. Attribution is `actor_user_id` on the envelope, always — 01-F63 refuses even an actor field inside a payload for exactly this reason, and 01-F61 refuses a bare PIN pad because two staff sharing a PIN become indistinguishable in an append-only ledger.
  - **Names are resolved at RENDER TIME, never written into events.** An event carries the id; the roster resolves the word. So a rename is an ordinary edit and not a correction of history — 01-F1 needs none, because nothing historical said the old name. The consequence is stated because the shortcut is tempting: a read model or a report that snapshots a name diverges from the roster silently and permanently, which is the same defect as re-deriving a price the ledger already captured (01-F53, inverted).
  - **A person record is never deleted.** 14-F14's deactivation is the exit: the record stays resolvable so every event it authored still renders a name, which is 01-F55's tombstone argument applied to the people axis. Erasure of a staff member's PII, if it is ever required, is 22-F14's mechanism and leaves the attribution standing.
  - **⚠ EXTENDED August 2026 (`plans/saas-pivot/plan-of-record.md` R23, R26).** Two of the fields this FR requires had no rule beyond their names, and both turned out to be load-bearing: the device-plane credential is `11-F21`, and the exit — the half this FR's *"never deleted"* sentence asserted and nothing enforced — is `11-F22`. Neither restates the minimum above; each decides what happens to one field of it.

- 11-F21 **THE CLOUD HOLDS AN ARGON2ID PIN HASH, THE ROSTER CARRIES IT TO DEVICES, AND IT IS NEVER A PIN AND NEVER LEAVES THE PROCESS THAT VERIFIES IT (August 2026, founder ruling R23).** `01-F28` requires PIN verification to work **on-device against synced credential hashes**, which needs a source; measured August 2026 there is none. The cloud user row is eight columns — id, org, email, display name, password hash, assignments, grid ordinal, created — and a comment-blind search of `services/` for a PIN credential returns nothing, while `packages/sync-client`'s `StaffMember.pin_hash` is real, verified against, and written by **one** caller: a development seed of three fictional people. So the verifier, the store and the lockout all shipped and the credential had no origin. Any answer that keeps PINs device-local was refused on its consequences rather than on taste: a cashier's PIN would work on one till and not the next, would be lost on re-pairing (`01-N5`), and `14-F14`'s manager reset would have nothing to write to.
  - **What is stored is a HASH and never a PIN**, on either plane: no procedure returns one, no payload carries one (`14 §2` already says PINs are never present in payloads), no log records one, and `14-F14`'s *"never displayed"* is unchanged. A PIN exists in exactly two places for exactly as long as each takes — the keypad it is typed on and the argument to a verify call.
  - **`01-F61`'s Argon2id cost floor governs it, and it is ONE declaration for both planes.** `01-F61` already rules two *bindings* of one algorithm with the same parameters (a native addon on the cloud plane, pure JS on the device plane, because `packages/domain` must stay importable by every workspace). The encoded hash is that FR's format, declared once in `domain`: a cloud writer and a device verifier that hash differently produce an offline refusal of a credential the owner has just set, and the till is where it would be discovered.
  - **It is a SECOND credential beside the back-office password (`15-F26`), not the same one.** `01-F26`/`01-F28` make the PIN the person's device-plane credential and `01-F27` keeps device tokens carrying **device** identity only; two credentials on two planes for one person record (`11-F20`) is the shape the corpus already has, and collapsing them would put a password on a shared terminal or a 4-digit convenience credential (`01-F61`) on the cloud plane.
  - **It reaches devices INSIDE the roster artifact** (`01-F75`), which is branch-scoped for this reason (`01-F76`): a hash a device does not hold cannot be verified with the WAN down, so `01-F28` forces the delivery. **The cost is accepted explicitly rather than discovered:** an unrevoked device holds the PIN hash of every **active** person its branch roster names.
  - **THE HASH IS CARRIED ONLY ON AN `active` ENTRY, AND THAT IS WHAT KEEPS THE BOUND A BOUND.** `11-F22` retains a departed person's row forever so her name still renders, and `11-F22` also says only `active` verifies a PIN — so a hash on a non-`active` entry is a credential **no verifier can ever reach**: pure blast radius with no function. Left in, the set grows monotonically with turnover until a branch till holds the Argon2id hash of everyone who has ever worked there, and a stolen or resold till is an offline attack surface against a credential `01-F61` itself calls *"a convenience credential, not a secret"*. R23 accepts the cost *"narrowed by R25"*, and this is the second narrowing available on the same axis. Rendering needs `user_id` and `display_name` only, which is `11-F22`'s own separation of the two facts. **Consequence for a protected path, stated so it is not discovered at a type error:** `packages/sync-client`'s `StaffMember.pin_hash` is required today, so the field becomes optional on the roster entry, and a **missing hash on a non-`active` member is the specified shape and never `malformed`** (`01-F75`) — a validator that refuses it refuses the whole update and is a branch that cannot sign in.
  - What bounds it is what already bounds a device — registration, expiry and revocation (`01-F25`, `01-F47`, `01-F42`, `01-F48`) — plus the branch scope, which is the half of the cost that can be bought down. ⚠ **One of those four is claimed and not enforced, and saying so is the point of listing them:** `01-F42`'s device-side purge has **no consumer** — the gateway produces `purge_command` and `packages/sync-client/src/cloud-session.ts:625` drops it at the default arm (*"ping/pong/purge unused here"*) — so a revoked till keeps whatever roster it holds indefinitely. The revocation half of this bound is therefore **OWED**, not shipped; an FR that claims a protection retires the assertion someone would otherwise write, which is why it is named here rather than assumed.
  - **IT MUST NOT CROSS THE IPC PLANE TO A RENDERER, AND THAT IS NOW REQUIRED RATHER THAN INCIDENTAL.** Both shipped hosts already **map** the roster rather than forwarding it — `apps/pos-electron/src/main/index.ts:1568` and `apps/pass-kds/src/main/pass-identity.ts:141` project `user_id`, `display_name` and (on the counter) this branch's assignment, and drop the hash — and `01-F28` puts verification in the process that holds the store. A renderer has no use for a credential: a host that forwarded the roster row unchanged would put every member's hash into a window's memory and into anything that can inspect one. **What makes this worth an FR rather than a code comment: measured August 2026, nothing aims an assertion at that projection** — the roster rows that appear in either host's suites are fixtures, no test names the IPC payload's shape, and the CI seam rail is blind to it by construction (nothing is unreached and nothing is unsupplied). The projection is a habit two files keep by choice; this clause is what a third host is built against.
  - **Not decided here, and BOTH halves block a surface rather than annotating one.** (i) Who chooses a person's first PIN and how it reaches her — `14-F14` puts set/reset in the back office; `15-F27`'s *"a password is never an input"* binds the vendor→owner leg and nothing binds owner→staff, and `28-F13` records that the corpus owns no outbound-mail capability at all (`14 §9.10`). (ii) **WHERE the hash lives on the cloud** — a ninth column on the existing user row read by two services, or a separate credential table with its own writer (§9.7). This FR rules **that** the cloud stores one (R23) and deliberately does not rule where; it is not a matter of taste, because the back-office login already reads `password_hash` off that row and putting a device-plane credential beside it widens what a login lookup returns.

- 11-F22 **A PERSON RECORD CARRIES A PARTICIPATION STATUS; IT DECIDES WHETHER SHE MAY ACT AND IT DECIDES NOTHING ABOUT RENDERING (August 2026, founder ruling R26).** `11-F20` says a person record is never deleted and `14-F14` says deactivation preserves historical attribution; neither names the field that carries it, so the device plane invented the opposite answer (below). The ruling: **a let-go cashier's name still renders on last month's orders.**
  - **THE NAME IS DELIBERATE AND NARROW: this is an ACCOUNT's participation, not a person's employment.** *Suspended*, *on leave*, *probation*, *notice period* are an employment lifecycle, and if this corpus ever specifies one it belongs to this module's own staff plane (`11-F1`..`11-F11` — attendance, advances, scheduling) and is a **different field**. `15-F25` is the near precedent and names the org's field for what it is (*"active ↔ suspended, and there is deliberately NO third state"*) without claiming to be a lifecycle. A two-value flag wearing an HR name is how the HR name gets taken.
  - **The statuses are `active` and `inactive`, and the set is closed at two.** `14-F14`'s word is *deactivation*; a wider vocabulary is org policy nobody has ruled, and inventing one here would be inventing policy (commandment 2). Widening the set is a spec act.
  - **Only `active` PARTICIPATES — and participation is not only the PIN check.** An inactive person does not unlock, on any device, WAN or no WAN; **and the authorization subject reads the status too**, so an inactive person authorizes nothing even from a session that predates her deactivation. ⚠ **This clause is written the wide way on purpose, because the narrow way is a live fail-OPEN.** Today `subjectOf` (`apps/pos-electron/src/main/authorize.ts:254`) builds `can()`'s subject from the roster row, so hard removal is what currently ends a person's authority — `assignments` becomes `[]` and the next write fails closed. `11-F22` retains the row, so a status that gated **only** the PIN check would hand every retained row its full assignments back and let a deactivated cashier keep recording payments, pay-outs and refunds — permanently, into an append-only ledger (`01-F1`, `02-F41`). Removing the mechanism that fails closed without replacing it is not a smaller change than the defect it fixes. `14-F14`'s *deactivation* and `01-F48`'s *"where state cannot be read, participation is refused, not granted"* both mean participation, and commandment 8 puts the check at the matrix.
  - **Status governs a PRESENT row and says nothing about an ABSENT artifact.** A roster a device cannot fetch, or one a week old, is `01 §9.6` — a different question, open, and deliberately not answered here: answering it in this FR would decide on the identity path, by accident, whether a branch can open at 05:00 with the WAN down. ⚠ **A migration consequence is OWED and named rather than ruled:** rows written by a build that predates this field carry no status, and the device's `display_name` optionality records what a strict validator does to a roster it does not recognise (*"a whole roster unable to unlock, which is `01-F17`'s stopped till arriving through a validator"*). The field is **required at the writer** (`01-F75`), so nothing on the wire lacks it; what a device does with its own older stored rows is a protected-path code decision owed against this FR, not a licence to default an absent status to `active`.
  - **Rendering is independent of it, permanently, and the record is retained FULL STOP.** `11-F20`'s rule is unqualified — *"a person record is never deleted"* — and this FR does not weaken it with a retention bound: `22-F13`'s matrix is normative and has **no row for the user directory or for reference data at all**, so a lifetime tied to event retention would be a number with no owner, and on a device `01-F14`'s rolling window prunes the very events that would have justified keeping her. So: every person the roster has ever named stays resolvable by `user_id`, and a past order, a reprint, a shift report and `02-F23`'s reconciliation render her `display_name` unchanged. That is `11-F20`'s render-time rule (an event carries the id, the roster resolves the word) and `01-F55`'s tombstone argument on the people axis. *(If a bound is ever wanted, it needs a `22-F13` row in the same change.)*
  - **The one rendering surface status DOES govern is the identification grid, and only because a control there is an offer.** `01-F61`'s grid offers **`active` members only**: a tile that always refuses is a control that cannot do its job, and hiding one moves nothing, because `01-F61` makes position an **explicit `grid_ordinal`** rather than a list index — which is exactly why that FR banned derived ordering. Every *other* rendering of a name — order, receipt, KOT, report, reconciliation — is unconditional. Stated because both answers are defensible and an implementer would otherwise pick one silently.
  - **A BRANCH TRANSFER IS EXPRESSIBLE, AND IT IS DERIVED HERE RATHER THAN LEFT ONE STEP SHORT.** A cashier who moves from branch A to branch B is not a departure and R25 makes the roster branch-scoped, so the case has to be answerable in two artifacts: she is **`inactive` in A's roster and `active` in B's**. She is not dropped from A, because R26's ruling is that her name renders on last month's orders and those orders are resolved by A's tills from A's artifact — dropping her degrades them to a raw UUID, which is the defect this FR exists to fix arriving through an internal transfer instead of a departure. She is not left `active` in A, because then her PIN unlocks a till she no longer works at and `11-F21`'s hash stays on A's devices forever. *(Which people a branch's artifact contains **in addition** to this is `01 §9.7` and is untouched: this clause fixes only the retention direction, which R26 settles.)*
  - **TWO FIELDS, NEVER ONE.** *May she act* and *does she render* are different questions with different failure directions — the first fails closed, the second fails to a raw UUID on a document a customer is holding — and one flag answers both, silently, by answering the first. This is also what fixes the wire shape: `01-F75` carries a marked entry and no removals list.
  - **The refusal needs a NAME, because the shipped vocabulary is closed and none of it fits.** `unlock()` returns exactly `device_not_registered` / `locked_out` / `unknown_user` / `bad_pin`; an inactive member is none of them — `unknown_user` is false (she is on the roster) and `bad_pin` is a lie about a PIN that was never checked. The reason is **`not_active`**, declared here because this is a `20 §4.4` protected path where a closed refusal vocabulary is part of the contract. ⚠ **It records no lockout failure, and under this FR that costs nothing rather than opening a brute-force path:** `unknown_user` already refuses without charging an attempt, and a non-`active` entry carries **no `pin_hash` at all** (`11-F21`), so there is no credential on the device to guess.
  - ⚠ **One claim was made here and withdrawn, because it was false and the false version is the more useful record.** An earlier draft said the retained row means *"`01-F61`'s per-(device, user) lockout counter never loses its subject"*. It does not: `pin_attempts` is its own table keyed `(device_id, user_id)` (`packages/sync-client/src/pin-attempts.ts:52-60`), is never joined to the roster, is untouched by a snapshot's `clearAll`, and `unlock()` reads it **before** the registry — so the counter never had a subject in the roster to lose and retaining the row changes nothing about it. This FR therefore makes **no** claim about the lockout. A protection asserted in prose retires the assertion someone would otherwise write, and that is worse than none.
  - **⚠ THE SHIPPED DEVICE CODE IS WRONG HERE AND THIS FR NAMES IT — a finding, not an insult.** `packages/sync-client/src/staff.ts:71` carries a `removals` list on the delta and `:207` clears the whole table on a snapshot, justified in a comment at `:60-71` that cites `01-F42`/`01-F48` for hard removal. **That citation is right about unlocking and wrong about rendering**, and the consequence is measured, not predicted: `apps/pos-electron/src/main/index.ts:777` degrades a member the registry no longer holds to a raw `user_id`, so a departed cashier's attribution reads as an opaque id everywhere it is shown. `11-F20`'s *"never deleted"* was true of the cloud record and unenforced on the device, which is exactly how a sentence and its code disagree for a whole wave. Both changes are on a protected path (`20 §4.4`) and are owed against this FR.

- 11-F23 **THE DEVICE-PLANE PIN HASH LIVES IN ITS OWN CREDENTIAL TABLE, NOT AS A COLUMN ON THE USER ROW (August 2026).** `11-F21` rules **that** the cloud stores an Argon2id PIN hash and deliberately does not rule **where**, recording in terms that this "is not a matter of taste". §9.7 posed it and nothing has answered it, and the first migration that writes the credential answers it by accident — which is the failure that clause exists to prevent. **⚠ THIS IS A PINNED INTERPRETATION, NOT A FOUNDER RULING**, and it is cheap to overturn: `plans/saas-pivot/plan-of-record.md` R4 puts nothing in the field, so schemas may change freely. Overturn it by amending this FR, not by writing the other shape in a migration.
  - **The argument is `11-F21`'s own, followed to its end.** That FR says a device-plane credential beside `password_hash` "widens what every login lookup returns, and `01-F71`'s enforcement points are all downstream of that lookup". `services/api`'s login path reads the user row by email and, on a ninth-column layout, would hold every logged-in owner's *cashiers'* PIN hashes in the memory of a request that has no use for them. A separate table means the login lookup cannot return the credential **because it does not join to it** — a structural bound rather than a discipline, which is the distinction `01-F60` already argues for a REQUIRED input and `01-F66` for a lock taken by the runtime rather than a file.
  - **It also expresses `11-F21`'s active-only rule as an absence rather than a NULL.** That FR carries the hash "only on an `active` entry", because a hash on a non-`active` entry is a credential no verifier can reach — pure blast radius with no function. A column makes that a nullable field every reader must remember to check; a table makes it **no row**, and the publisher's `left join` produces the specified shape without a branch. `01-F75` requires a missing `pin_hash` on a non-`active` member to be the specified shape and **never `malformed`**, so the two representations differ in how easily a validator gets that wrong.
  - **Its own writer, which is the half that matters for `14-F14`.** PIN set/reset (`14-F14`) and back-office password set (`15-F26`) are two credentials on two planes for one person (`11-F21`), and giving them one writer is how a password reset comes to touch a PIN by refactor. Separate table, separate writer, separate `14-F39` `user.manage` authorization on the way in.
  - **What this does NOT decide, so a migration does not decide it silently:** the table's retention when a person is deactivated. `11-F20` never deletes the PERSON record; this FR says nothing about whether the credential row is deleted, retained, or retained-and-unreachable when status leaves `active`. The three differ on what happens when she is re-activated — a PIN that still works, a PIN that must be re-set, or a lockout row that outlives both — and `14-F14`'s reset path is the surface that would have to say so. Owed to doc 14, and named here because deleting the row is the obvious implementation and is not obviously right.

## 4. Key flows

**Flow A — Morning clock-in**
1. Staff member unlocks any branch device with their PIN (the session they already need to work).
2. Inside a scheduled shift window, the device prompts "Clock in?" — one tap; selfie captured if org-enabled.
3. `staff.clocked_in` persists locally before the confirmation shows (01-F2); syncs whenever connectivity allows.
4. *Offline:* identical experience; event syncs later.
5. *Camera dead:* selfie skip recorded with a flag; manager sees the flagged entry — service is never blocked.
6. *Forgot entirely:* no clock event exists; the schedule shows the absence; the manager records a correction event next day if warranted (append-only, attributed).

**Flow B — Advance from the drawer**
1. Manager starts a paid-out (doc 02) and picks category `staff_advance`.
2. Selects staff member + amount (+ optional repayment plan); confirms with manager PIN.
3. Device is handed to the staff member, who sees the amount (large numerals) and acknowledges with their own PIN.
4. `cash.paid_out` + linked `staff.advance_recorded` written together; both ledgers update; both parties can see it forever.
5. *Refusal:* staff declines to acknowledge → nothing is recorded; the flow cannot complete one-sided.
6. *Advance outside the drawer* (owner's pocket): same dual-PIN flow started from the advances screen, without the `cash.paid_out` leg.

**Flow C — Payroll export (monthly ritual)**
1. Owner opens the export in doc 12/14 and picks the period.
2. Per-staff summary shown: days present, shifts, flagged overtime spans, advances taken, outstanding balance.
3. System proposes scheduled deductions from repayment plans; owner confirms or adjusts per staff member.
4. `staff.advance_repaid` events written; CSV generated and delivered (signed URL) for the accountant.
5. Each affected staff member gets a pending-acknowledgment badge at next PIN session → `staff.advance_acknowledged`.
6. *Dispute:* staff declines acknowledgment → dispute flag raised to owner; resolution is a correction event carrying both acknowledgments (11-F11).

**Flow D — Opening checklist**
1. Opener unlocks a device; today's opening checklist is the first tile.
2. Items checked one by one; photo captured where the template requires it.
3. Each `checklist.item_checked` streams to the manager console over the LAN fast path.
4. All items done → `checklist.completed`; gaps remain visible to manager/owner in real time.
5. *Not completed by open:* flagged on doc 05/12; never blocks opening the day.

**Flow E — Shift handover**
1. Cashier runs the normal shift close (doc 02); the handover form appears with cash figures prefilled from the close.
2. Pending issues added as quick-tags (+ optional text or voice note); stock notes as quick-tags.
3. `handover.recorded` attached to `shift.closed`.
4. The next shift's first PIN unlock on that station shows the handover note before anything else.

**Flow F — Schedule publish**
1. Manager copies last week's template in doc 05/14 and edits.
2. Explicit publish → `staff.schedule_published`; schedule distributes as reference data.
3. Staff devices show each person their own upcoming shifts; changed entries are marked.
4. *Unpublished edits* are invisible to staff — there is no half-published state.

## 5. Data

- **Entities owned (cloud read models, rebuildable per 01-F7):** `attendance_records` (folded from clock events), `advance_ledger` (running balances), `schedules` + `shift_presets`, `checklist_templates` + `checklist_runs`, `handover_notes`, `sop_documents` (metadata; files in object storage), `payroll_exports` (generated artifacts).
- **Events emitted (from 01 §4):** `staff.clocked_in / clocked_out / advance_recorded / advance_repaid`.
- **Events added to the 01 §4 catalog by this spec:** `staff.schedule_published`, `staff.advance_acknowledged`, `checklist.item_checked`, `checklist.completed`, `handover.recorded`.
- **Events consumed:** `cash.paid_out`, `shift.opened/closed`, `day.opened/closed`, PIN session context (01-F26).
- Selfie/checklist photos: object storage, compressed, refs on events; retention per §9.

## 6. Non-functional requirements

Cross-cutting NFRs inherited from 00 §5. Module-specific:

- 11-N1 Clock-in ≤ 5 s end-to-end including selfie on reference hardware; ≤ 3 taps.
- 11-N2 Selfie/checklist photos compressed client-side ≤ 150 KB; capture never blocked by connectivity; deferred upload.
- 11-N3 Payroll export generated < 10 s per branch-month.
- 11-N4 Checklist item checks and clock events reach the in-branch manager console over the LAN fast path < 1 s (00 §5.3).
- 11-N5 SOP cache on branch devices capped at 100 MB, LRU-evicted, outside the kernel budget of 01-N3.
- 11-N6 Advance-balance query on owner app and staff self-view served from local/cached read model < 1 s.

## 7. Customizability

- **Layer 1 (platform admin):** module feature flag (Wave 3 staged rollout).
- **Layer 2 (org):** shift presets and lengths; overtime threshold; selfie on/off; advance-granting roles and per-role advance cap without owner approval; default repayment fraction; checklist templates incl. photo-required items; handover and stock quick-tag sets.
- **Layer 3 (branch/device):** preferred attendance surface per branch; checklist template assignment per branch.
- **Deliberately not configurable:** staff self-view of own attendance and balance (always on); the dual-PIN requirement on advances; append-only ledger; absence of salary computation; schedules never auto-create attendance facts.

## 8. Tech notes

- Backend `people` module in the modular Node backend; read models Postgres/Drizzle; no new apps — surfaces are screens inside docs 02/03/05 hosts, reusing the existing PIN session identity (01-F26), so there is no second login system.
- Camera: `expo-camera` on Android hosts (18 §14 registry), `getUserMedia` on Electron; photos through the same deferred upload queue as doc 10 invoices.
- Voice notes: Opus, 30 s cap, object storage; playback on manager/owner surfaces.
- Schedules/checklist templates ship as reference-data snapshots + deltas (01 §8) — no bespoke sync.
- Exports produced by the jobs service; delivered as signed URLs; CSV schema versioned in `packages/domain`.

6. ~~**Does a till-only cashier need an email address? — BLOCKING**~~ **RULED (founder, August 2026, R30): NO — a cashier who only uses the till needs no email; email is required only for BACK-OFFICE access.** The question, kept because the reasoning still binds what is built: `11-F20`'s required minimum does not list one and `11-F21` gives her a PIN as her working credential, but the cloud user table makes email `NOT NULL` and unique **case-folded and globally**, so a person record could not be written without one and a CRUD (`14-F14`) would have to invent one per cashier. **That inventing is what the ruling refuses:** many cashiers have no address, and an owner made to supply one puts a wrong address permanently into a directory `11-F20` never deletes from. **Consequences, stated so they are not discovered at a migration:** `email` becomes **nullable** (`services/sync-gateway/src/schema.ts` declares it `notNull` today, with a unique index on `lower(email)`; Postgres permits multiple NULLs in a unique index, so the index survives unchanged), and the back-office login path must never assume an email exists — `findByEmail` is the lookup, and a till-only person is simply **not findable by it**, which is correct rather than a gap. `28 §9.18`'s global-uniqueness rule is untouched: this ruling removes the requirement to *have* an address, not the rule about two people sharing one.
7. ~~**Where the device-plane PIN hash lives on the cloud**~~ **ANSWERED by `11-F23` (August 2026): its own credential table, not a ninth column — as a PINNED INTERPRETATION rather than a founder ruling, overturnable by amending that FR and cheap to overturn because R4 puts nothing in the field.** The question as posed, kept because `11-F23`'s argument is built on it: a ninth column on the existing user row, read by both `services/api` and the roster publisher, or a separate credential table with its own writer? `11-F21` rules **that** the cloud stores one (R23) and deliberately does not rule where. It is not a matter of taste: the back-office login already reads `password_hash` off that row, so a device-plane credential beside it widens what every login lookup returns, and `01-F71`'s enforcement points are all downstream of that lookup. Recorded here because it was asked as part of the same ruling and answering it silently in a migration is how a security-relevant layout gets chosen by whoever typed first.
8. ~~**Whether deactivating the person who is CURRENTLY signed in must end her live session**~~ **RULED (founder, August 2026, R27): HER SESSION ENDS TOO. The BOUND is still open and is deliberately not borrowed.** R27 rules that a removal takes effect **immediately** while every other roster change still defers to `01-F46`'s 05:00 boundary, and the live-session half follows by necessity rather than by preference: *a deactivation that leaves the person signed in is not immediate in the only case that matters.* The reasoning this question recorded still stands and is what the ruling was made against: `11-F22` already makes the authorization subject read status, so from the moment a roster applies she can do nothing — every write refuses — and today `unlock()` sets a user that only an explicit lock or `01-F26`'s idle expiry clears, so the till would go on showing her name on the honesty strip while refusing everything she touches. **What is still open and must not be filled in by analogy:** `01-F48`'s 30 s eviction bound is written for a **device**, and a session is not one, so borrowing that number would be inventing it (commandment 2). The two costs the ruling weighed are both real and unchanged — an immediate sign-out mid-transaction drops a cashier out of a part-rung order, and leaving the session up presents a working till that refuses every act with no explanation — so **what the surface does with a part-rung order at the moment the session ends is owed to doc 02**, not answered here.
